# How to Save for a Big Purchase Without Guessing Each Month

The laptop still works, as long as the charger stays at exactly the right angle. You know you’ll need to replace it. You’ve even checked the price a few times.

But “I should save for that” hasn’t yet become an amount you put aside.

A big purchase is easier to plan when you give it a name, a realistic cost and a place in your regular budget. You can then see whether the purchase fits your timeline—and what needs to change if it doesn’t.

Here’s how to build that plan without making every month revolve around it.

## 1. Price the whole purchase

Start with what you actually expect to pay. For a laptop, that might include delivery, an essential adapter or software you need straight away. For a course, it might include materials as well as the enrolment fee.

Separate essentials from extras. A protective case you need and a premium accessory you’d simply like don’t have to enter the plan together.

Use a current quote or price when possible. If the cost is uncertain, label your target as an estimate and revisit it before buying. A neat savings total is only useful if it still covers the purchase.

## 2. Subtract what is already set aside

Count money already reserved for this purchase. Keep money committed to rent, bills or another goal out of the calculation.

Suppose the full purchase will cost 1,200 and you already have 300 set aside. You need another 900.

That remaining amount is the starting point for your contribution plan:

**Amount still needed = purchase target − money already reserved**

If you track several goals, check that you haven’t allocated the same savings to two of them. A single amount of 300 can’t fully fund both a laptop goal and a holiday goal at the same time.

## 3. Turn the remaining amount into a regular contribution

Choose a contribution schedule that fits when you receive money: weekly, monthly or around your pay cycle.

Divide the amount still needed by the number of contributions you can make before the purchase. Count actual opportunities to put money aside; “six months away” doesn’t always mean six paydays remain.

Here’s a fictional example. Every amount uses the same currency, and the calculation assumes no interest or returns.

| Part of the plan | Amount |
| --- | ---: |
| Total purchase target | 1,200 |
| Already reserved | 300 |
| Still needed | 900 |
| Contributions remaining | 6 |
| Amount per contribution | 150 |

The [Consumer Financial Protection Bureau’s savings-plan worksheet](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_your-money-your-goals_savings_plan_tool_2018-11_ADA.pdf) uses this approach of dividing a savings amount across the weeks available. You can apply the arithmetic to the contribution schedule you use.

## 4. Check whether that amount fits your budget

The calculation tells you what the timeline requires. Your budget tells you whether it’s workable.

Look at known bills, everyday spending and other commitments before adopting the contribution amount. If 150 would leave the period short, revise the plan now.

For example, if 100 fits more comfortably, the remaining 900 would take nine contributions rather than six. You could move the purchase date, reduce the purchase cost or reconsider another optional commitment.

There’s no benefit in choosing an impressive target that you already know you can’t maintain. A smaller contribution with a realistic date gives you a plan you can use.

If you’re still unsure what your ordinary spending looks like, start with [tracking daily expenses](https://paisatrace.hirubix.com/blog/how-to-track-daily-expenses). Those records can help you decide how much room the goal has.

## 5. Track the contribution when it happens

Planning to save 150 and actually setting aside 150 are different events. Keep both visible.

In PaisaTrace, open **Money Plan → Goals → Add goal** to enter a name, target and currency. A contribution plan for the selected period is optional. Record money you put aside with **Record savings** when the movement happens.

If you’ve already recorded an eligible savings contribution, use the existing-entry options described in the [savings goals guide](https://paisatrace.hirubix.com/guide#goals). Linking an existing entry assigns that record to the goal without creating another transaction. Don’t link a contribution already included in the goal’s starting savings.

PaisaTrace tracks these records; it does not move money or establish your actual bank balance. Its goal progress combines starting savings and linked contributions, less withdrawals, across periods.

## 6. Adjust from your actual progress

At the end of a contribution period, compare the plan with what you put aside.

If you saved less, use the current shortfall and remaining contribution dates to recalculate. If you saved more, check whether you want to bring the purchase forward or leave the original date in place.

For instance, after two contributions of 100 in the example above, total progress would be 500: the original 300 plus another 200. You would still need 700. With four contribution dates left, that would mean 175 each—or a revised deadline.

Recheck the purchase price, too. The target may need changing even when your contributions are going to plan.
